Issue 06

Hand impairment with phlegm-stasis pattern: a 200-person, four-arm study of a glove plus electroacupuncture

Putting a robotic glove and electroacupuncture in one plan is a specific integrative choice, and the result has to stay attached to the arm that produced it. A 2022 four-arm study in the Journal of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine enrolled 200 people with stroke hand impairment of the phlegm-stasis pattern and compared glove plus electroacupuncture, glove alone, electroacupuncture alone, and usual rehabilitation. The paper’s own “total effective rates” are 88%, 76%, 77.6%, and 70.8%. That composite is the authors’ summary. It is not a product efficacy rate.

Background

Under this pattern diagnosis, pathways often combine modern rehabilitation with electroacupuncture. A four-arm layout can show combination, device alone, acupuncture alone, and usual care on one scale, instead of a before-after story with no contrast.

A total effective rate collapses several judgments into one percentage. It is useful for a coarse contrast and useless as a person’s change on a named scale. It cannot be quoted as “the glove’s efficacy is 88%.”

Study overview

Study design

Four arms: robotic glove plus electroacupuncture, glove alone, electroacupuncture alone, and usual rehabilitation. The outcome discussed here is the paper’s total effective rate. Author affiliations include Shanghai Siyi Intelligent Technology, with Yin Ganggang as a coauthor. That disclosure stays with the reading.

Sample and schedule

Two hundred people with hand impairment after stroke in the phlegm-stasis pattern. This is the largest sample in the column. The paper does not name a model that can be matched to a current product, and the generic photograph is not such a match.

Main findings

The reported total effective rates are 88% for glove plus electroacupuncture, 76% for the glove alone, 77.6% for electroacupuncture alone, and 70.8% for usual rehabilitation. A higher composite in the combined arm means only that, under this study’s rule, that arm scored higher. It does not assign 88% to the glove, and 76% is not a product rate. The glove-alone and electroacupuncture-alone figures sit close together, which also argues against explaining the whole pattern by one component.

What this study adds

Two hundred people and four arms give a contrastable composite, not a percentage for a product page. Read as a total effective rate, the gaps between combination, single modalities, and usual care are limited.

The paper, as summarized here, does not supply the same mean differences used in the column’s other trials. Until those scales are on the table, the study is about how the components were combined, not about how many points a hand score will rise.

Source

Limits The percentages are the study’s own total effective rates. The combined arm cannot be attributed to the glove or to electroacupuncture alone. No model is named.
