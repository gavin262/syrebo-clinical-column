Supplement 07

A pneumatic source without electronics: bench pressure, flow, temperature, and noise

From the paper

Low-Heat and Near-Silent Pneumatic Source Driven by Integrated Endothermic–Exothermic Chemical Reactions for Soft Robots

Ung Heo, Junhwan Choi, Sangjoon J. Kim, Jung Kim

Soft Robotics, OnlineFirst, 2026

DOI: 10.1177/21695172261442062

View source

Paper figures: reuse not confirmed; none are reproduced on this page.

The paper proposes a way to supply gas to a soft robot: an integrated endothermic and exothermic reaction, with no electronic parts, regulated by passive mechanical components that follow the state of the system. The abstract reports a steady output of 7 bar and 6 L/min, a peak temperature of 36°C, and noise of 40 dBA. The subject is a gas source, not hand function, and not a claim that a rehabilitation product has been shown to work. The publicly reviewed abstract does not identify a Syrebo product; whether the full text demonstrates a specific brand or model remains unverified.

Background

A soft structure moves when it has gas. The size, heat, noise, and control electronics of a pump limit the room a device can sit in. Another way to supply gas is an engineering question.

Pressure and decibels on a bench describe a source specimen. Behavior of a whole device while a person uses it is a different test. This page repeats the performance sentences in the abstract and does not add fabrication steps.

Study overview

The abstract describes gas generated by a reaction of phosphoric acid and cesium bicarbonate, with endothermic and exothermic processes placed together to limit heat. Passive mechanical parts regulate the reaction as the system changes state. There is no electronic controller. The measurements are output, temperature, and noise. Study design

Sample and schedule

The abstract reports measurements of the experimental source rather than outcomes in a patient group.

Main findings

The abstract reports steady pressure of 7 bar, steady flow of 6 L/min, a peak temperature of 36°C, and noise of 40 dBA. Those figures describe the tested source under the authors’ reported conditions; they are not specifications for a Syrebo product. They are not a clinical safety conclusion or a change in hand function.
