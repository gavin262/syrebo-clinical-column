Issue 12

Subacute upper-limb scores: a three-arm pilot of the SY-HR06 and mirror therapy

From the paper

Combination of robot-assisted glove and mirror therapy improves upper limb motor function in subacute stroke patients: a randomized controlled pilot study

Qian J, Liang C, Liu R, Yu J, Yang T, Bai D

Frontiers in Neurology, 2025;16:1602896

DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1602896 · ChiCTR2200057613

View source

The study compared mirror therapy, robotic glove training, and the combination of both in three parallel arms. The study used the Syrebo SY-HR06. Among people who completed analysis, the combined arm had a higher post-treatment upper-limb motor score than the mirror-therapy arm. That arm received two treatments at once, so the difference cannot be written as the glove alone. Training lasted four weeks, with no follow-up.

Brand note: The paper calls the device Yisheng SY-HR06. Yisheng and Syrebo are the company’s Chinese and international brands, respectively.

Background

Upper-limb practice moves between watching a movement and making one. Mirror therapy uses the unaffected limb’s reflection to organize attention. A pneumatic glove repeats opening and closing of the affected hand. The two can be scheduled apart or in the same session. Those are different pathways.

A sentence that only says the score changed after a device was added will be read as a device effect. The three-arm design puts mirror therapy, the glove, and the combination on the same page. It is still a pilot. It asks whether a larger trial is worth doing. It does not show that a result lasts.

Holding a cup, fastening a button, or keeping a pen depends on separated finger movement, and walking often returns sooner. In the subacute period the nervous system is still changing, so a score gap can include spontaneous recovery. A therapist reading a pilot checks which training paths were compared, whether the assessor knew the assignment, and the week the observation stopped. This paper places mirror therapy, the glove, and both together in one design so a combined score is not assigned to one of those pieces.

Study overview

The study used a Syrebo SY-HR06 pneumatic soft glove on the affected hand for clench-and-relax practice. All three arms also received usual rehabilitation. Outcomes included FMA-UE, Brunnstrom stages for the upper limb and the hand, the FTHUE-HK, and the Functional Independence Measure. A certified occupational therapist who did not know the assignment did the assessments. The photograph in this column is a brand scene, not a photograph of the study device.

The intervention is three sentences. The mirror arm grasped while watching the unaffected upper limb. The glove arm wore the Syrebo SY-HR06 on the affected hand and practiced clench and relax. The combined arm scheduled the glove and mirror therapy in the same session. All three arms kept usual rehabilitation. The paper describes 30 minutes of usual rehabilitation and a further 30 minutes of the assigned study intervention, each five times a week for four weeks.

Outcomes, by the names used in practice: motor impairment with the upper-limb Fugl-Meyer assessment (FMA-UE); recovery stage with Brunnstrom stages for the upper limb and the hand; functional tasks with the Hong Kong version of the Functional Test for the Hemiplegic Upper Extremity (FTHUE-HK); independence in daily activity with the Functional Independence Measure (FIM). A certified occupational therapist who did not know the assignment did the scoring.

Study design

Single-blind randomized controlled pilot with three parallel arms. The mirror arm practiced grasp while watching the unaffected upper limb. The glove arm used the SY-HR06 for open-and-close practice. The combined arm did glove practice and mirror therapy in the same session. Registration: ChiCTR2200057613.

Sample and schedule

The paper’s results section reports that 100 people were screened and 66 were randomized. Fourteen left during the intervention and were excluded from the analysis. The analyzed sample was 16 in mirror therapy, 18 in the glove arm, and 18 in the combined arm: 52 in total. The paper’s abstract describes 52 as randomized, so this difference requires clarification. The paper describes 30 minutes of usual rehabilitation and a further 30 minutes of the assigned study intervention, each five times a week for four weeks.

Main findings

The paper reports that several scores changed from each arm’s own baseline. For the between-arm comparison, post-treatment FMA-UE was 37.61±11.09 in the combined arm and 23.63±12.69 in the mirror arm; the paper gives p=0.006 for that comparison. In the combined arm, post-treatment Brunnstrom stage was 4.06±0.87 for the upper limb and 4.67±1.24 for the hand, and the Functional Independence Measure was 94.17±9.49. The abstract reports these as higher than in the mirror arm. The clearest abstract comparison is combined versus mirror, not a claim that the combined arm beat the glove-only arm on every outcome.

What this study adds

The study put visual feedback and repeated movement of the affected hand in one session, and it kept the separate pathways in the same design. What can be discussed is how a therapist orders mirror therapy, glove practice, or both, on top of usual rehabilitation.

The combined score cannot be rewritten as the effect of the glove by itself, and four weeks cannot be rewritten as a lasting result. The paper discusses differences in side-related distribution between groups; the precise description of side needs clarification. Grasp counts were not compared. These details belong next to the p value.

What can be discussed in scheduling is whether mirror therapy and repeated opening of the affected hand are written as one session or as two, and that those are different plans to record. The record names the path, the weeks, the withdrawals, and whether the assessor knew the assignment. The combined score does not go into a description of the glove alone, and four weeks is not a lasting result.

Source

Limits Pilot scale, with 16 to 18 people analyzed per arm. Four weeks and no follow-up. Fourteen withdrawals were excluded from the analysis. The paper discusses differences in side distribution, and active repetitions were not counted. The p value is the paper’s report for that one comparison. Counts, scores, and the p value are quoted from the paper. They are not converted into a percentage gain and they are not a product claim.
