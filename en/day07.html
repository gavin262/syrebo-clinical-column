Issue 07

Hand rehabilitation after middle-cerebral-artery infarction: a case report of one 66-year-old patient

From the paper

Effectiveness of Syrebo's Glove Rehabilitation Program in a Patient With Middle Cerebral Artery Infarct: A Case Report

Sharma VS, Sharath HV, Sasun AR

Cureus, 2024, 16(4): e59314

DOI: 10.7759/cureus.59314 · PMID: 38817453 · PMC11136872

A randomized trial describes an average difference. A case report describes how rehabilitation was arranged for one person. In 2024 Cureus, Sharma and colleagues reported a 66-year-old woman with infarction in the middle-cerebral-artery territories who received neurophysiotherapy that included a Syrebo glove program. The report follows the course of observation and functional change. It is one case, and it does not generalize.

Background

Infarction in this territory often affects the arm and hand together with language or cognition, so the plan is more than a weak hand. Families usually need to know when glove practice sat inside physiotherapy, and who supervised it, more than they need a percentage.

A case report can write that sequence down. It has no parallel control, so any before-after change mixes spontaneous recovery, usual care, and the glove. Those pieces cannot be separated.

Study overview

The paper discusses a Syrebo glove program and does not name a model. The glove-and-console photograph is Figure 2 of that open-access report (CC BY 4.0), shown in the excerpt at the top. It is not a figure from a different review.

Study design

A single case report, not a randomized trial. After the person was medically stable, clinicians delivered a six-week neurophysiotherapy program. The Syrebo glove was listed among the outpatient rehabilitation interventions in weeks 3–6.

Sample and schedule

A 66-year-old woman with bilateral MCA-territory infarction. The report records observations and functional change. It does not provide between-group statistics.

Main findings

The value of the report is the course of care, not a rate of improvement. It shows how a soft glove was placed inside rehabilitation for one person with a severe infarct, under professional supervision. A change in a single case is not a promised degree of recovery.

What this study adds

Among a column that is mostly randomized trials, one case report is here to show how a program was used for a person. It fills a narrative gap. It does not raise the level of evidence.

One case cannot estimate how often adverse events occur, and it cannot say whether the acute, subacute, or chronic phase is the better fit. Those questions stay with controlled studies.

Source

Limits Single case. Not a general effect. Functional change cannot be assigned to the glove alone.

Source: Sharma VS, Sharath HV, Sasun AR. Effectiveness of Syrebo's Glove Rehabilitation Program in a Patient With Middle Cerebral Artery Infarct: A Case Report. Cureus, 2024, 16(4): e59314. DOI: 10.7759/cureus.59314 (PMID: 38817453).
