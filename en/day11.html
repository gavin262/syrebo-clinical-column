Finger robots intended for home use: a development note in the Journal of the Robotics Society of Japan

Home hand rehabilitation has been discussed for years. In 2023 the Journal of the Robotics Society of Japan published an article by Furudate and colleagues about developing home rehabilitation robots with clinicians. The authors mentioned a Syrebo hand glove as one commercially available example in their discussion of finger rehabilitation. This is related research and is not a clinical trial of a Syrebo device.

Background

Between the ward and the home sit donning, safety limits, and use when no therapist is in the room. Engineering papers put those constraints inside the work with clinicians. Being named means the authors chose this product as an example. It does not mean the society ran a controlled study.

The authors' 2023 observation that relatively few home-use devices had reached practical use describes the context at the time; it does not establish the current market.

Study overview

The glove-and-console image in this column is Figure 2 of the 2024 Cureus case report by Sharma and colleagues (CC BY 4.0). It is not a figure from the Japanese review.

Study design

This review discusses rehabilitation-robot development with clinicians, including the authors' work on finger rehabilitation and frailty prevention. It does not describe a clinical trial of a Syrebo device.

Sample and schedule

The article discusses development work and refers to other research; it does not present a clinical sample for a Syrebo product trial.

Main findings

The authors noted in 2023 that relatively few home-oriented products had reached practical use and cited a Syrebo glove as one example. Their article discusses rehabilitation-robot development; it does not establish the efficacy of a Syrebo product.

What this study adds

The review discusses how clinicians can inform the development of home rehabilitation robots. It cites a Syrebo glove as a commercial example while describing the authors' own projects separately.

Whether it can be used safely and repeatedly at home still depends on indication, supervision, and separate clinical studies. The society paper does not provide those data.
