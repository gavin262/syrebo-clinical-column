Issue 08

Upper-limb impairment after stroke: a 60-person randomized study of innovative mirror therapy

From the paper

创新式镜像疗法对脑卒中患者上肢功能障碍的康复效果

陈涛、刘沙鑫、陈雪莲、毛磊、李德波、干林玉

新医学, 2024, 55(6): 397-402

DOI: 10.3969/j.issn.0253-9802.2024.06.001

View source

Conventional mirror therapy uses a mirror to suggest that the affected hand is moving. The innovative version asks a robotic glove to carry the affected hand through the same action made by the unaffected hand, so seeing and doing share the session. In 2024 New Medicine published a randomized study by Chen and colleagues that compared the two versions in 60 people over four weeks. The paper reports larger gains for innovative mirror therapy on upper-limb movement, wrist-hand movement, and selected upper-limb-related daily activities.

Background

Mirror therapy has a known limit: if the affected hand does not move, the visual suggestion has no paired motor input. Connecting assisted flexion and extension to the mirror task is an attempt to have the affected side actually complete a movement inside a range the therapist sets.

Whether that change shows up on scores has to be tested against conventional mirror therapy, not against doing nothing. Sixty people, split evenly, is a preliminary randomized size.

Study overview

The paper identifies the hand rehabilitation device used in the study as the SY-HR08, made by the company behind Syrebo. Yisheng is the company's Chinese brand; its international brand is Syrebo.

Study design

Random-number allocation. Innovative mirror therapy, combining a mirror task with a robotic glove, was compared with conventional mirror therapy. Both groups also received conventional rehabilitation before their assigned mirror therapy. Outcomes included FMA-UE, FMA-WH, and five upper-limb-related items from the modified Barthel Index.

Sample and schedule

Sixty people with upper-limb impairment after stroke, 30 in each group, for four weeks.

Main findings

The paper reports greater improvement for innovative mirror therapy on FMA-UE, FMA-WH, and the selected modified Barthel Index items. That is the study’s between-group description. Means and P values are not recalculated on this page. Those figures stay with the paper.

What this study adds

The question is whether adding an assisted movement that matches the mirror changes scores relative to conventional mirror therapy in the rehabilitation programs studied. Sixty people provide a preliminary contrast, not a home-program proof.

The paper identifies the study device as the SY-HR08. The score difference is as reported in the paper.

Source

Limits Thirty people per group. Differences follow the paper. Means and P values are not recomputed here. The study device model is stated in the paper. The paper describes 20 minutes of conventional rehabilitation plus 20 minutes of mirror therapy but elsewhere calls each mirror-therapy session 40 minutes; its eligibility criteria and reported disease durations also differ. These details require clarification.
