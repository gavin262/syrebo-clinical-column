Issue 09

Left hemiparesis after MCA infarction: one record of faradic stimulation plus a robotic hand

From the paper

Rehabilitation of Left-Sided Hemiparesis Post-MCA Infarction Using Faradic Electrical Stimulation and Robotic Hand Rehabilitation: A Case Report

Prabhu C, Mohan P, Sajjana D

International Journal of Physiotherapy Research and Clinical Practice, 2025, 4(2): 27-30

IJPRCP, 2025, 4(2): 27-30

View source

Syrebo C12 powered glove and controller (cropped from the official C12 product page photo on syrebocare.com)

A hemiparetic hand is sometimes given two inputs at once: electrical stimulation aimed at the muscle, and a robotic hand to open and close the fingers. A 2025 case report in the International Journal of Physiotherapy Research and Clinical Practice records four weeks of faradic stimulation plus a Syrebo C12 for one man with left hemiparesis. It is a record of a combined plan, not an efficacy trial of either piece.

Background

Months after stroke, hand change is usually slower, and clinicians sometimes add stimulation or external assistance to ordinary practice. Once both are used, a before-after change cannot be given cleanly to either one.

The report is still useful. It states when the plan started, how often it ran, and which device was in the room. It does not give a rate, and it does not give a difference against a control.

Study overview

The robotic device in the report is the Syrebo C12. The photograph is a crop from the official C12 product page so the controller and glove can be recognized. It does not add specifications the paper omitted.

Study design

A single combined intervention: faradic stimulation together with Syrebo C12 robotic hand practice. There is no parallel control group.

Sample and schedule

A 60-year-old man with left hemiparesis. The plan began 3 months and 2 weeks after stroke, five times a week, for four weeks.

Main findings

This page does not reprint functional scores that have not been checked against the paper for the column. What is clear is the arrangement: stimulation and robotic practice were used together, so a change cannot be written as the effect of the C12 alone or of stimulation alone.

What this study adds

The record adds a concrete schedule: about three months after stroke, five sessions a week, two modalities together. It does not raise the level of evidence.

One left-hemiparetic case after MCA infarction does not stand in for other lesions or other times after onset.

Source

Limits Single combined case. Change is not assigned to the C12 or to stimulation, and it is not a general effect.
