Soft Robotic Glove in Early Subacute Stroke: Feasibility Evidence for the SY-HR03E

SY-HR03E hospital/clinic-grade soft rehabilitation glove (main photo from the official product page on syrebocare.com)

The early subacute phase after stroke is a critical window for hand rehabilitation—but patients are still fragile, so whether a new glove can be used safely must be answered first. A pilot randomized study was designed to do just that.

How the study was done

Twenty patients with early subacute stroke were randomly assigned to two groups of 10, receiving SYREBO SY-HR03E soft robotic glove training over 4 weeks, for a total of 20 sessions. The study authors include affiliations with Shanghai SiYi Intelligent Technology.

What it found

No dropouts or adverse events occurred, supporting feasibility in this fragile phase. On efficacy signals, FM-Hand (hand motor function) and the Modified Barthel Index (daily living ability) showed a significant group-by-time interaction—meaning the glove group improved more over time than the control group.

Limitations

Pilot study (n=20); conclusions are limited to feasibility and preliminary efficacy.

Source: Lin Y, Wang C, Yin G, et al. Feasibility and preliminary efficacy of a soft robotic glove for hand rehabilitation in early subacute stroke: a pilot investigation. BioMedical Engineering OnLine, 2026. DOI: 10.1186/s12938-026-01614-6 (Clinical Trial Registry: ChiCTR2000034614).
Note: Rehabilitation outcomes vary by individual. Please follow the training plan prescribed by your physician or therapist.
