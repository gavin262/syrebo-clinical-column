Supplement 02

Fire needle plus robotic-glove mirror therapy: 110 people analyzed over four weeks

From the paper

火针联合机器人手套镜像疗法对脑卒中后手及上肢功能障碍影响

张伟、黄文婷、崔晓阳、郑婵娟、陈焱强

亚太传统医药, 2024;20(9):73–78

DOI: 10.11954/ytctyy.202409014

View source

Paper figures: reuse not confirmed; none are reproduced on this page.

SYREBO brand scene (HR03E). Not a photograph of the SY-HR03Y in the paper.

One hundred thirteen people were allocated by a random-number table. Three left, and 110 were analyzed. Both arms received usual medication and rehabilitation, and both used the Yisheng SY-HR03Y from Shanghai Siyi Intelligent Technology for mirror training. The observation arm also received fire needle. After four weeks that arm was higher on several measures. The gap belongs to fire needle plus the glove, not to the glove by itself.

Background

A mirror glove records the unaffected hand and drives the affected one. Fire needle is a separate intervention, with its own visits and minutes. When both arms wear the same glove, the between-arm test is about the added piece, not about the glove against a program that never uses one.

The first question for this design is whether the control arm already contains the device. It does. The subject of the conclusion is then the combination.

Study overview

The study was done in the rehabilitation department of Hubei Provincial Hospital of Integrated Chinese and Western Medicine, among people admitted from July 2022 to July 2023 with hand and upper-limb limitation after stroke. The control arm used the SY-HR03Y: a data glove on the unaffected side drove the affected side. A flexion-extension mode was followed by a ball-grasp mode, once a day, five days a week, for four weeks. The observation arm added fire needle, about five minutes a session, three times a week, for four weeks, on top of the same glove program. The photograph is a brand scene, not the SY-HR03Y in the paper.

Study design

Allocation by a random-number table. Both arms had usual medication, usual rehabilitation, and SY-HR03Y mirror training. The observation arm also received fire needle. This compares glove-plus-needle with glove, not glove with an empty program.

Sample and schedule

Of 113 people allocated, two left the observation arm and one left the control arm. Fifty-five people per arm, 110 in total, were analyzed. Follow-up stopped at four weeks.

Main findings

The paper reports that after four weeks both arms rose on peak torque, average power, total work, Fugl-Meyer score, and the modified Barthel index, while root-mean-square values fell, and that the observation arm was higher, with the paper’s P<0.05. Hand Fugl-Meyer moved from 7.53±1.64 to 18.42±2.71 in the observation arm and from 7.24±1.30 to 13.67±2.26 in the control arm; the post-treatment between-arm p is printed as 0.000, which is read as p<0.001, not as a probability of zero. Flexion peak torque moved from 7.26±2.13 to 13.17±2.34 versus 7.01±2.55 to 10.01±3.26. Extension peak torque moved from 6.88±2.49 to 11.26±2.18 versus 6.32±2.40 to 9.02±2.51. The co-contraction ratio did not show a clear change.

What this study adds

The full text supports discussing the glove mirror program as one piece inside a combined path. Because both arms used the glove, it does not support a claim that the glove alone beat the control. The presence of fire needle is the between-arm difference.

Source

Limits One month and no follow-up, so the scores cannot be treated as lasting. The sample is from one center; the authors ask for a larger one. The observation arm had an extra intervention, so the gap cannot all be assigned to the glove. A printed p=0.000 is read as p<0.001.

Source: Zhang Wei, Huang Wenting, Cui Xiaoyang, Zheng Chanjuan, Chen Yanqiang. Effect of fire needle combined with robotic-glove mirror therapy on hand and upper-limb impairment after stroke. Asia-Pacific Traditional Medicine, 2024;20(9):73–78. DOI: 10.11954/ytctyy.202409014.
