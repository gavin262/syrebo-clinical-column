Supplement 02

Fire needle plus robotic-glove mirror therapy: 110 people analyzed over four weeks

From the paper

火针联合机器人手套镜像疗法对脑卒中后手及上肢功能障碍影响

张伟、黄文婷、崔晓阳、郑婵娟、陈焱强

亚太传统医药, 2024;20(9):73–78

DOI: 10.11954/ytctyy.202409014

One hundred thirteen people were allocated by a random-number table. Three left, and 110 were analyzed. Both arms received usual rehabilitation and robotic-glove mirror therapy. The observation arm also received fire needle. After four weeks that arm was higher on several measures. The comparison concerns adding fire needle to the shared program; it does not establish the effect of the glove by itself.

Background

A mirror glove records the unaffected hand and drives the affected one. Fire needle is a separate intervention, with its own visits and minutes. When both arms wear the same glove, the between-arm test is about the added piece, not about the glove against a program that never uses one.

The first question for this design is whether the control arm already contains the device. It does. The subject of the conclusion is then the combination.

Practice for the hand and upper limb after stroke often includes medication, usual rehabilitation, and one hand program at the same time. When both arms already do the same glove training, the between-arm comparison answers what the extra piece adds. It does not answer the glove against a program that never uses one. Fire needle has its own visits and minutes, so the subject of the sentence is the combination.

Study overview

The journal abstract describes a randomized study in people with hand and upper-limb impairment after stroke. Both groups received usual rehabilitation and robotic-glove mirror therapy; the observation group also received fire needle. Outcomes were assessed after four weeks. The photograph is a brand scene rather than a confirmed photograph of the study device.

Both groups received usual rehabilitation and robotic-glove mirror therapy. The observation group added fire needle. The publicly available abstract does not identify the glove manufacturer or model.

Outcomes by their clinical names included the hand motor score (hand Fugl-Meyer), peak torque in flexion and extension, average power, total work, root-mean-square, the co-contraction ratio, and the modified Barthel index for daily activity. The photograph is an illustrative brand scene, not a confirmed image of the study device.

Study design

Allocation by a random-number table. Both arms had usual rehabilitation and robotic-glove mirror therapy. The observation arm also received fire needle. This compares glove-plus-needle with glove, not glove with an empty program.

Sample and schedule

Of 113 people allocated, three withdrew. Fifty-five people per arm, 110 in total, were analyzed. The abstract reports assessments after four weeks.

Main findings

The journal abstract reports that after four weeks both arms improved on peak torque, average power, total work, hand Fugl-Meyer score and the modified Barthel Index, while root-mean-square values decreased. The observation arm had higher reported scores on the first measures and lower root-mean-square values than the control arm. The authors report no clear change in the co-contraction ratio. Detailed values require full-text review.

What this study adds

The journal abstract describes the glove mirror program as one piece inside a combined path. Because both arms used the glove, this comparison does not establish that the glove alone beat the control. The observation arm also received fire needle.

If a service schedules glove mirror training and another therapy in the same weeks, the record should say whether both arms used the glove. The abstract reports that, with robotic-glove mirror therapy in both arms, the arm that added fire needle was higher on several measures after four weeks. It cannot support writing that gap as the glove alone; longer-term follow-up needs full-text review.

Source

Limits The journal abstract reports results after four weeks; longer-term follow-up and additional study limitations need full-text review. The observation arm had an extra intervention, so the gap cannot be assigned to the glove alone. The results are not rewritten as a product percentage.
