Issue 02

Post-stroke hand function: a single-blind RCT of a soft robotic glove and mirror therapy, with 6-month follow-up

From the paper

Soft robotic gloves versus mirror therapy: a long-term comparative study on hand function and motor recovery in post-stroke rehabilitation

Abdelraouf OR, et al.

Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2025

DOI: 10.2340/jrm.v57.43482 · PMID: 40874348 · NCT05898971

SY-M1 + E12 mirroring training photo; the device shown is E12, the study device model is as stated in the source

Mirror therapy uses visual input to recruit the affected side. A soft robotic glove uses compliant actuation to repeat opening and closing. Which change lasts longer cannot be answered at the last training day. A 2025 single-blind randomized trial in the Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine compared the two approaches for eight weeks and followed people to six months. The paper reports statistically significant between-group gains for the glove on the Box and Block Test, grip strength, and FMA-UE at the end of training, and reports that those glove-group gains were still present at six months.

Background

The question that matters after hand training is whether the change is still there after people go home. Mirror therapy is simple and depends on attention to the reflected limb. A glove shifts repetition onto an external actuator, with a different demand on attention and endurance. Both are rehabilitation methods. This is not a ranking of brands.

A score on the final training day can confuse a short stimulus with a stable change. A randomized comparison with a six-month visit is what makes that distinction possible.

Study overview

The column photograph shows an E12 mirror-training setup. The study device is whatever the paper specifies; the photo is not a model match.

Study design

Single-blind randomization of a soft robotic glove against mirror therapy. Outcomes were grasp-and-release dexterity (Box and Block), grip strength, and upper-limb movement (FMA-UE), with a visit six months after training.

Sample and schedule

Forty-nine people after stroke were randomized. Training lasted eight weeks. The registry entry is NCT05898971.

Main findings

At the end of training the paper reports between-group differences favoring the glove: Box and Block P=0.004, grip P=0.011, FMA-UE P=0.021. At six months the glove group’s gains were still present. Those P values describe this sample. They do not assign a recovery size to every patient.

What this study adds

The extra information is time. The between-group difference had not disappeared at the follow-up visit. That is more useful clinically than a ranking on the last training day.

Forty-nine people is still a small trial. Baseline differences, lesion side, and practice after discharge can all move a six-month score. The finding belongs to this population and this contrast.

Source

Limits Small sample (n=49). P values and maintained gains are the study’s report, not a product promise.

Source: Abdelraouf OR, et al. Soft robotic gloves versus mirror therapy: a long-term comparative study on hand function and motor recovery in post-stroke rehabilitation. Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2025. DOI: 10.2340/jrm.v57.43482 (PMID: 40874348; ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05898971).
