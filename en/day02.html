Soft Robotic Glove vs Mirror Therapy: 6-Month Evidence for Stroke Hand Rehabilitation

From the paper

Soft robotic gloves versus mirror therapy: a long-term comparative study on hand function and motor recovery in post-stroke rehabilitation

Abdelraouf OR, et al.

Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2025

DOI: 10.2340/jrm.v57.43482 · PMID: 40874348 · NCT05898971

SY-M1 + E12 mirroring training photo; the device shown is E12, the study device model is as stated in the source

Mirror therapy is a classic approach in stroke hand rehabilitation, while a soft robotic glove uses air-driven actuation to help fingers repeatedly practice grasping and release. Which one lasts longer? A single-blind randomized controlled trial with a 6-month follow-up offers an answer.

How the study was done

Forty-nine post-stroke patients were randomly assigned to a soft robotic glove group or a mirror therapy group for an 8-week intervention, with follow-up at 6 months, comparing hand function and motor recovery.

What it found

At the end of the intervention, the glove group showed statistically significant between-group improvements on three measures: the Box and Block Test (hand dexterity and grasp-and-release, P=0.004), grip strength (P=0.011), and the Fugl-Meyer Assessment–Upper Extremity (FMA-UE, P=0.021). Importantly, the gains were maintained at the 6-month follow-up, suggesting the benefit lasted beyond the training period.

Small sample (n=49); findings apply to the enrolled population.

Source: Abdelraouf OR, et al. Soft robotic gloves versus mirror therapy: a long-term comparative study on hand function and motor recovery in post-stroke rehabilitation. Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2025. DOI: 10.2340/jrm.v57.43482 (PMID: 40874348; ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05898971).
