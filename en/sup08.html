Measuring finger flexion: the instrument was built at a university; the brand appears only in the introduction

From the paper

Validity and reliability of an air-guitar for measuring finger flexion in healthy participants

P. Kuvijitsuwan, J. Klaphajone, P. Singjai, T. Kumpika, N. Thawinchai, C. Angkurawaranon, C. Aramrat, K. Utarachon

Scientific Reports, 2024;14:20113

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-71094-y

View source

This paper cannot be entered on the same list as a direct experiment with the brand. The apparatus was an air-guitar built at Chiang Mai University, used to measure static finger flexion in healthy adults. SYREBO appears as an example of a commercial device in the introduction. The paper’s value is agreement with a goniometer and retest behavior of that university-built instrument, not the performance or the clinical effect of any glove.

Background

A search on a brand name hits introductions, discussions, and reference lists. A hit means the authors wrote the name. The apparatus named in the methods is the device this study actually used.

Counting a secondary mention as a product study breaks the link between a catalog and the hardware that was on the bench. This paper is in the supplement so that boundary stays visible.

Study overview

The instrument was made at Chiang Mai University. It is not a SYREBO product. The study compared it with a Jamar finger goniometer and repeated the measures on the same day, morning and afternoon. The system resolution is given as 0.01°, while a goniometer is usually read to 5°. The measure is static maximum flexion, not a three-dimensional movement. The figure in the excerpt is original Figure 1 and shows that study’s own apparatus.

Study design

A validity and retest-reliability study in healthy adults. The comparison instrument was a finger goniometer. There is no treatment allocation and no training course.

Sample and schedule

Ten healthy, right-handed, asymptomatic adults, mean age 34.6 years, eight men and two women. The sample does not extend to patients or to dynamic grasp.

Main findings

Agreement with the goniometer had a concordance correlation of 0.62–0.90, with a mean difference of about 1°. Retest intraclass correlation for the air-guitar was 0.82–0.99, the standard error of measurement was 1°–3°, and the 95% minimal detectable change was 1°–9°. Those figures describe the university-built instrument. They cannot be rewritten as validation of a branded device.

What this study adds

The reason to include the paper is the difference between mentioning a brand and experimenting with that brand. The apparatus named in the methods outranks a product list in the introduction.

Source

Limits Ten healthy adults only. Static maximum flexion only, with no three-dimensional motion standard. The apparatus was built at a university. This is not a direct clinical or engineering study of SYREBO.
