Supplement 08

Measuring finger flexion: a study of a university-developed research device

From the paper

Validity and reliability of a finger training tool for assessing metacarpal phalangeal joint ranges of motion in asymptomatic participants

P. Kuvijitsuwan, J. Klaphajone, P. Singjai, T. Kumpika, N. Thawinchai, C. Angkurawaranon, C. Aramrat, K. Utarachon

Scientific Reports, 2024;14:20113

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-71094-y

View source

This paper cannot be entered on the same list as a direct experiment with the brand. The apparatus was an air-guitar built at Chiang Mai University, used to measure static finger flexion in healthy adults. SYREBO appears as an example of a commercial device in the introduction. The paper’s value is agreement with a goniometer and retest behavior of that university-built instrument, not the performance or the clinical effect of any glove.

Study overview

The instrument was made at Chiang Mai University. It is not a SYREBO product. The study compared it with a finger goniometer and repeated the measures on the same day, morning and afternoon. The system resolution is given as 0.01°, while the Jamar finger goniometer used in this study was read in 5° increments (the paper also says that when the reading falls between marks, 2.5° is read; the exact reading rule needs clarification). The measure is static maximum flexion, not a three-dimensional movement. The figure in the excerpt is original Figure 1 and shows that study’s own apparatus.

The apparatus was built at Chiang Mai University to measure static maximum finger flexion. It is not a glove from this brand. The comparison instrument was a finger goniometer. Measures were repeated on the same day, morning and afternoon. System resolution is given as 0.01 degrees, while the Jamar finger goniometer used in this study was read in 5-degree increments (the paper also says that when the reading falls between marks, 2.5° is read; the exact reading rule needs clarification). The figure in the excerpt is original Figure 1 and shows that study’s own apparatus.

The outcomes are measurement properties, not treatment outcomes: agreement with the goniometer, retest correlation, the standard error of measurement, and the 95% minimal detectable change. The participants were 10 healthy, right-handed, asymptomatic adults.

Study design

A validity and retest-reliability study in healthy adults. The comparison instrument was a finger goniometer. There is no treatment allocation and no training course.

Sample and schedule

Ten healthy, right-handed, asymptomatic adults, mean age 34.6 years, eight men and two women. The sample does not extend to patients or to dynamic grasp.

Main findings

Agreement with the goniometer had a concordance correlation of 0.62–0.90, with a mean difference of about 1°. Retest intraclass correlation for the air-guitar was 0.82–0.99, the standard error of measurement was 1°–3°, and the 95% minimal detectable change was 1°–9°. Those figures describe the university-built instrument. The paper notes that some individual measurements varied more widely and cautions that the instrument may not replace a clinical goniometer. They cannot be rewritten as validation of a Syrebo device.

What this study adds

The reason to include the paper is the difference between mentioning a brand and experimenting with that brand. The apparatus named in the methods outranks a product list in the introduction.

The paper is included so that a brand search starts with the apparatus named in the methods. Agreement and retest behavior belong to that university-built instrument. They cannot be rewritten as validation of a branded device, and they do not extend to patients or to dynamic grasp.
