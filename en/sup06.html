Amplitude-mode ultrasound as an interface for a soft hand exoskeleton: five healthy adults

From the paper

Ultrasound-Based Human–Machine Interfacing for Soft Hand Exoskeletons under Assisted and Passive Conditions

Bruno Grandi Sgambato, Juan C. Maldonado-Mejía, Tristan Wen Jie Choo, Marcela Múnera, Camilo A. R. Díaz, Majid Taghavi, Dario Farina, Carlos A. Cifuentes

2026 11th IEEE RAS/EMBS International Conference on Biomedical Robotics and Biomechatronics, pp. 495–500

DOI: 10.1109/BIOROB66782.2026.11681602

View source

Paper figures: reuse not confirmed; none are reproduced on this page.

The study asks whether amplitude-mode ultrasound can serve as an interface for a soft hand exoskeleton and detect grasp intent under active and passive conditions. An institutional-repository fragment says five healthy adults completed seven grasps drawn from a standardized assessment protocol. It is an engineering feasibility study, not an outcome study in people after stroke. Accuracy and the device brand have not been checked in the full text. Numbers from other ultrasound papers are not carried over.

Background

Surface electromyography is a familiar intent signal. Amplitude-mode ultrasound is another route: forearm echoes used to infer muscle state. For a soft exoskeleton the question is the interface, how the machine knows what the hand is about to do.

Healthy adults completing prescribed grasps in a laboratory show that a procedure can run. They do not show the same stability in daily use, and they do not show that a named brand of glove was in the experiment.

Study overview

The paper is about using forearm signals from amplitude-mode ultrasound for a soft hand exoskeleton, and about active versus passive conditions. The tasks were seven grasps. The participants were healthy adults. The material in hand is not enough to confirm the full cross-validation procedure, recognition accuracy, or the brand and model of the exoskeleton. This page does not show a product photograph.

Study design

An engineering experiment on a human-machine interface, comparing grasp-intent detection from ultrasound under active and passive conditions. It is not a randomized treatment trial and it does not compare functional scales over a course of care.

Sample and schedule

Five healthy adults and seven grasps. There is no patient sample, so there is no treatment course to describe.

Main findings

What can be confirmed is an exploration of intent detection under active and passive conditions, at the scale of five people and seven grasps. Accuracy and the statistical results wait on the full text. Accuracy figures published in other papers are not copied onto this one.

What this study adds

Amplitude-mode ultrasound is an intent path that is not surface electromyography. Its value here stops at interface feasibility. Moving from healthy volunteers to daily use still needs work on usability, stability, and safety. Until a brand is written into the methods, the study is not counted as an experiment on a named product.

Source

Limits Five healthy adults only. Not evidence of a patient outcome. The full text was not available; brand, model, and the complete performance figures are unverified. Not counted as a confirmed device experiment.
