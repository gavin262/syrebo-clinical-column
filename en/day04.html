Issue 04

Robotic Training Gloves: Between-Group Evidence on Grip Strength and Fugl-Meyer Scores

Generic soft rehabilitation glove illustration; the source does not specify a model, so no model correspondence is claimed

For stroke patients with poor hand function, two things matter most: being able to grip, and being able to move accurately—one is about strength, the other about motor control. A randomized controlled trial examined both.

How the study was done

Thirty stroke patients were randomly assigned to two groups of 15, comparing robotic training glove-assisted training with a control group.

What it found

Between-group differences were statistically significant on both measures: grip strength showed a mean difference of 1.87 (95% CI 0.39–3.35, P=0.015), and the Fugl-Meyer motor function score showed a mean difference of 16.67 (95% CI 3.9–29.4, P=0.012). In other words, the robotic glove group improved more than the control group on both strength and motor function.

Limitations

Small sample (n=30); between-group differences are mean differences.

Source: Abd Elhady AS, Ahmed GM, Hassan A, Ibrahim SM, Abdelmageed SM. Efficacy of robotic training gloves in improving hand function and movement in stroke patients. SPORT TK-EuroAmerican Journal of Sport Sciences, 2025, 14, Article 40. DOI: 10.6018/sportk.660321.
