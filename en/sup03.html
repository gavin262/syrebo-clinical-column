Supplement 03

Robotic-glove task-oriented mirror therapy: 30 people, eight weeks, an unblinded trial

From the paper

Effects of robotic glove-assisted task-oriented mirror therapy on hand motor function in sub-acute stroke patients: Randomised controlled trial

Abdul Rehman, Furqan Ahmed Siddiqi, Saman Shaukat

Hong Kong Physiotherapy Journal, 2026

DOI: 10.1142/S1013702526500149

View source

No figures from the paper are shown in this paper card.

Thirty people in the subacute period were randomized to robotic-glove-assisted task-oriented mirror therapy or to task-oriented mirror therapy alone. After eight weeks the abstract reports within-group and between-group differences on the Box and Block Test, FMA-UE, and ARAT. The trial was not blinded, and the sample is small. Exact p values and effect sizes in the abstract were not fully readable, so this page does not supply them. The accessible publisher abstract does not identify the device manufacturer or model.

Background

Task-oriented mirror therapy is already a program. Adding a glove asks whether one more device, on that program, is followed by a further difference on the scales. It is not a comparison against no hand practice at all.

Without blinding, the people delivering care and the assessment setting can carry expectations. With 30 people, a sentence that says “statistically significant” still needs the effect size from the full text before anyone can say how large the gap was.

Task-oriented practice places the exercise inside an action that resembles daily use. Mirror therapy uses the unaffected limb’s image to organize attention. When those two are already one program, adding a glove asks whether one more device is followed by a further difference on the scales. An unblinded design and a sample of 30 people mean a sentence that says “a difference” is not yet a confirmatory result.

Study overview

Arm A was glove-assisted task-oriented mirror therapy. Arm B was task-oriented mirror therapy. Both arms had 30 minutes of usual care and then 30 minutes of the assigned intervention, once a day, five days a week, for eight weeks.

Arm B was task-oriented mirror therapy. The accessible publisher abstract does not identify the glove manufacturer or model; the complete paper is needed to verify the device.

The three outcomes by their clinical names: the Box and Block Test (BBT) for picking up and moving blocks; the upper-limb Fugl-Meyer assessment (FMA-UE) for motor impairment; the Action Research Arm Test (ARAT) for grasp, grip, pinch, and gross movement.

Study design

Unblinded randomized comparison with sealed envelopes. The question is task-oriented mirror therapy with or without a robotic glove. Outcomes were the Box and Block Test (BBT), FMA-UE, and the Action Research Arm Test (ARAT).

Sample and schedule

Thirty people. One session a day, five days a week, for eight weeks. The full text was not available, so completion, adverse events, and follow-up are not inferred.

Main findings

The abstract reports change in both arms and a between-group difference described as statistically significant. The three scales cover grasp-and-place, motor impairment, and upper-limb activity. Because the publisher abstract did not show a complete p value, this page does not write one, and it does not write an effect size. The device manufacturer and model remain to be verified in the complete paper.

What this study adds

The question is close to a clinical choice: when task-oriented mirror therapy is already in the plan, does the abstract report a difference after a glove is added. The public sentence can say that the abstract reports a between-group result, and it has to carry the unblinded design, the small sample, and the unchecked full text in the same breath.

What a therapist can discuss from the abstract is whether, once task-oriented mirror therapy is already on the timetable, adding a glove was reported as a between-group difference. Any public sentence has to carry the unblinded design, the 30 people, the unchecked effect sizes, and the unconfirmed model. A p value that could not be read is not filled into a handover or a product sheet.
