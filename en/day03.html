Issue 03

Unilateral neglect in subacute stroke: a randomized comparison of robotic mirror training

From the paper

Effects of Robotic Mirroring Training and Mirror Therapy with Robot on Unilateral Neglect, Depression, and Activities of Daily Living Among Patients with Subacute Stroke

Woo JJ, Kwon JS

NeuroRehabilitation, 2025

DOI: 10.1177/10538135251382902 · PMID: 41092018

View source

Syrebo E12 series hand rehabilitation glove system with controller, power glove and mirror glove (official photo; study device: SY-HRE12)

Some people in the subacute phase have more than a weak hand. They may also neglect one side of space, and mood may suffer with it. Robotic mirror training maps the less-affected hand onto the affected one, so vision and movement arrive in the same repetition. A 2025 randomized trial in NeuroRehabilitation compared two ways of using the same soft glove: its mirror function, and a preset passive mode. The paper reports an advantage for mirror training on neglect-related and depression-related outcomes. The sample is 24 people.

Background

Neglect makes scores hard to read. The problem is not only that the hand will not lift; attention to one side of space is reduced. A conventional mirror can suggest that the affected hand is moving when it is not. Robotic mirroring tries to make the affected glove move with the other hand, so seeing and doing occupy the same trial.

If the groups used different devices, any difference could be the hardware. This trial gave both groups the SY-HRE12 and changed only the mode. The question is how the glove was driven, not whether a new machine was present.

Study overview

The paper used the SYREBO SY-HRE12. The photograph shows an E12 controller, powered glove, and mirror glove so the system is recognizable. The study device is the SY-HRE12.

Study design

Randomized comparison. Both groups used the SY-HRE12. One group used the mirror function. The other used preset passive motion. Outcomes included unilateral neglect, depression-related measures, and daily activities.

Sample and schedule

Twenty-four people in the subacute phase, 12 in each group. Sessions lasted 30 minutes, three times a week, for five weeks.

Main findings

In this sample the paper reports an advantage for robotic mirror training over preset passive motion on neglect-related and depression-related outcomes. That is a difference between two modes of one device. It does not mean the glove treats neglect or depression. The control condition was not an ordinary mirror box.

What this study adds

The shift in question is from “whether to use a glove” to “which mode of the same glove.” For people who also have neglect and mood burden, that is a more precise clinical question.

Twenty-four participants support only a cautious reading. The trial does not test unsupervised home use, and it does not offer a guaranteed change in attention or mood.

Source

Limits Small sample (12 per group). The control mode was preset passive motion, not a mirror box. Differences are the study’s report.
