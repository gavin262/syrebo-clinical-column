Issue 03

Robotic Mirroring Training: Letting the Healthy Hand Guide the Affected Hand

Syrebo E12 series hand rehabilitation glove system with controller, power glove and mirror glove (official photo; study device: SY-HRE12)

After a stroke, some patients develop unilateral neglect—losing awareness of one side of space—alongside emotional difficulties. Robotic mirroring training takes a different approach: the healthy hand's movements drive the affected hand to imitate them in real time, combining visual and motor feedback during repetitive practice.

How the study was done

Twenty-four patients with subacute stroke were randomly assigned to two groups of 12. Both groups used the SYREBO SY-HRE12 soft robotic glove. The robotic mirroring training group used the device's mirroring function, while the other group used pre-programmed passive movements. Sessions lasted 30 minutes, three times per week, for 5 weeks.

What it found

Robotic mirroring training showed greater advantages on unilateral neglect and depression-related outcomes. In other words, with the same glove, "mirrored practice" offered an extra benefit on attention and mood compared with passive practice.

Small sample (n=24); the control was preset passive movement, not a conventional mirror box.

Source: Woo JJ, Kwon JS. Effects of Robotic Mirroring Training and Mirror Therapy with Robot on Unilateral Neglect, Depression, and Activities of Daily Living Among Patients with Subacute Stroke. NeuroRehabilitation, 2025. DOI: 10.1177/10538135251382902 (PMID: 41092018).
Note: Rehabilitation outcomes vary by individual. Please follow the training plan prescribed by your physician or therapist.
